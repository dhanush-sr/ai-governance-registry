AI Governance Registry with Deployment Gates

Can this AI be trusted
with people's loans?

A bank wants an AI to sort loan applications. EU law calls that high risk: before it's used, it must be documented, fair, traceable and approved.

Our system checks every AI automatically, and blocks any that fails.

How it works

Every AI goes through the same checkpoint

1 · Team builds an AIand fills in its info sheet: what it's for, its limits, who approved it
→
2 · Uploads it to GitHubthe training script also saves a "seal" (fingerprint) of its test results
→
3 · Our checker runsautomatically, on every change, using a written rule book
→
4 · Decision✓ Released or ✗ Blocked, with the reason
1
How risky is it?

Worked out from what it's used for, using EU law

2
Is the paperwork complete?

Every section the rule book requires

3
Can we trace where it came from?

Data and results match their saved fingerprints

4
Is it fair to every group?

Re-measured by the checker, never self-reported

5
Did someone independent approve it?

Signed off by a person who didn't build it

The rules come from the EU AI Act and an existing fairness standard (the "four-fifths rule"), not from us. The checker never takes the team's word: it works things out itself.

Bank-sized volumes

Checking 25 million applications

A real bank scores millions of applications, so the fairness check runs on Apache Spark, which splits the work across many processor cores at once.

Normal way (1 core)
85 s
Spark, 1 core
92 s
Spark, 2 cores
47 s
Spark, 4 cores
29 s
Spark, 8 cores
20 s

4.3× faster on 8 cores, and the fairness score came out identical at every size. The 25 million rows are the 195,665 census people copied with new IDs, which tests speed, not new findings.
